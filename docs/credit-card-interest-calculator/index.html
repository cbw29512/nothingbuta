How it works

Credit cards show an annual percentage rate, which is the yearly interest rate. Most cards charge interest monthly, so the calculator divides the yearly rate by 12 and multiplies it by your balance to estimate one month of interest.

Worked example

Example: You carry a $5,000 balance at a 24.99 percent annual percentage rate.

Monthly rate: 24.99 divided by 12 is about 2.083 percent.

One month of interest: $5,000 times 0.020825 is about $104.13.

Twelve months at the same balance: about $1,249.50.

Paying more than the interest each month is the only way the balance goes down.

Common questions

Why is my real interest charge a little different?

Most cards use your average daily balance and the number of days in the billing cycle. This is a simple monthly estimate.

How do I avoid credit card interest?

Pay the full statement balance by the due date each month. Most cards then charge no interest on purchases.

How long will it take to pay off my card?

Use the debt payoff calculator to see payoff time and total interest.
